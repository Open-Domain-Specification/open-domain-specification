# A bad ref survives a round trip; an external context has no internal events; the process lifecycle exemption is exact

Three leftovers from card 100. A workspace with an unresolvable reference now loads with a diagnostic, but `toSchema` writes the unset link, so open-and-save drops the typo silently. `external-is-boundary` refuses an internal operation on an external context but not an internal event, which is the same invention. And `isProcessLifecycle` exempts any ring whose only reactor is a process, including rings that leave the context and come back through a neighbour's operation, which is wider than "a process fed by its own steps" and hid one shape of the two-caller defect.

- **senior-developer** (2026-09-09T21:05:00.000Z): What a bad ref still cannot survive, and why. Three writes are not kept, because the element that would hold them is not there afterwards: a consumption whose `consumable` names nothing (the consumption is the pair it joins, so it is dropped and reported at the consumer), a relationship whose end names nothing (same, dropped whole), and an entity or value object relation whose `target` names nothing (same). Keeping those means keeping a half-built consumption, relationship or relation in the model for every rule downstream to trip over, which is a bigger change than this card and a decision for the lead rather than for me. A consumption's `by` is the fourth: the consumption survives, but the miss is recorded at the consumer's ref, which is where the diagnostic wants to land, and moving it to the consumption's own ref would move the diagnostic with it. And a `returns` keeps its ref but not its `many`, which says how many of a shape a call comes back with and means nothing until the shape resolves. All four are written down on `UnresolvedWrites` and in `keepUnresolvedRefs`, so the next reader is not left guessing what is covered.
