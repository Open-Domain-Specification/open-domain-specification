- **ironhide** (2026-09-05T01:30:00.000Z): The rule. `identifies-root` (error) fires when the named entity is not its aggregate's root, at the attribute's own ref, because that is what the author edits. The reason in the comment and in the catalogue is the DDD one: the root is what an aggregate is reached, loaded and saved by, so an id of a part inside someone else's boundary names something nobody outside can ask for — which is also what makes an identity safe to cross a context at all. It parses no text and reads no type string. `schema-context` needed no exemption: it only looks at `attribute.schema`, and a test asserts that an identity naming a root in another context reports nothing from `identifies-root`, `cross-context-relation` or `schema-context` together.
