## Five whys and process lessons

1. Why did the original postCI fail after local and preCI success? One availability wait timed out after the page committed but before the expected shell message was observed. The CI log proves the symptom; the controlled stall demonstrates a sufficient mechanism, not the actual cause.
2. Why could the two observations have different timing? Router/page commit and window.postMessage delivery occur in separate tasks; one fixed wait budget spanned both stages.
3. Why was the test exposed? It asserted only the last posted state and did not first observe committed page identity. The correction observes both stages and asserts the whole exact message sequence.
4. Why did readiness miss this axis? Focused, full and native proof validated behavior but did not force event-loop delay at the observation boundary. No claim that all loaded runners or all interleavings are now proven.
5. What control closes this gap? For tests of sequential async stages, wait for each observable prerequisite before timing the next stage, keep strict final/sequence assertions, and prove a relevant regression is still rejected. Use deterministic local evidence before CI reruns; do not invent product recovery from hypotheses.
